<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As a personal injury attorney specializing in cases involving children&#8217;s injuries, I often come across stories that highlight the dangers of corporate negligence and how the media can twist the narrative to protect big businesses at the expense of everyday people. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Recently, a post on X (formerly Twitter) resurfaced the infamous 1992 McDonald&#8217;s hot coffee case involving Stella Liebeck, a 79-year-old woman who suffered horrific burns. While the headlines at the time painted it as a greedy lawsuit over a simple spill, the truth is far more sobering – and it has direct parallels to the child injury cases I handle every day. Let&#8217;s set the record straight on what really happened. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Stella was in the passenger seat of a parked car when she accidentally spilled a cup of McDonald&#8217;s coffee on her lap while trying to add cream and sugar. The coffee, served at a scorching <strong>180-190°F</strong> (far hotter than typical home-brewed coffee), caused third-degree burns over 16% of her body, including sensitive areas like her thighs and groin. She needed extensive skin grafts, spent weeks in the hospital, and dealt with permanent scarring and mobility issues. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Initially, she <strong>only sought $20,000</strong> to cover her medical bills, but McDonald&#8217;s refused, leading to a trial that ended in a verdict that delivered a resounding rebuke to McDonald&#8217;s.  </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">During the proceedings, it emerged that McDonald&#8217;s had received <strong>over 700 complaints </strong>about burns from their coffee in the previous decade, yet they <strong>continued to serve it at dangerously high temperatures to cut costs</strong> on refills and extend shelf life. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The jury awarded Stella compensatory damages for her suffering and punitive damages equivalent to about two days of McDonald&#8217;s coffee profits – a total of around $2.9 million, which was later reduced. So in the end, after reductions and attorney fees, Stella didn&#8217;t receive anything close to a windfall.  </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The case wasn&#8217;t about a clumsy spill; <strong>it was about a company knowingly putting profits over safety</strong>. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Stella&#8217;s case resonates deeply with me because child burn injuries from hot liquids are alarmingly common. According to the American Burn Association, scalds from hot beverages account for a significant portion of burn injuries in children under 5, often requiring hospitalization and long-term care. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Just like in Stella&#8217;s case, these incidents aren&#8217;t always &#8220;accidents&#8221; – they can stem from negligence, such as restaurants serving overly hot drinks without proper warnings, or products designed without child safety in mind.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But here&#8217;s the bigger issue: the media dishonesty around the hot coffee lawsuit fueled a wave of &#8220;tort reform&#8221; propaganda, portraying personal injury claims as frivolous get-rich-quick schemes. This has made it harder for families to seek justice when their children are hurt due to someone else&#8217;s carelessness. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Parents are ridiculed for suing over playground defects, defective car seats, or unsafe toys, even when the evidence shows clear corporate shortcuts that endangered kids. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>These lawsuits aren&#8217;t about windfalls; they&#8217;re about accountability, compensation for medical bills, lost wages, and pain, and preventing future harm.</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Take, for example, cases involving hot beverage spills on children in fast-food drive-thrus or at home with poorly designed mugs. <strong>If companies aren&#8217;t held responsible, they have little incentive to change – just as McDonald&#8217;s resisted safer practices until Stella&#8217;s lawsuit forced their hand.</strong> </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">By debunking myths like the &#8220;hot coffee lady who became a millionaire,&#8221; we can shift the focus back to protecting the vulnerable, especially our children. </p>
